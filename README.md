An end-to-end data analytics project analyzing BlinkIT's grocery sales data. It covers data cleaning, exploratory analysis, statistical hypothesis testing, a Random Forest feature-importance model, K-Means outlet segmentation, and a live interactive HTML5 dashboard.

---

## 1. Project Objective

BlinkIT operates multiple grocery outlet types across different city tiers. The central question this project answers:

**"What factors drive BlinkIT's product sales, which products and outlets perform best, and what should BlinkIT do differently?"**

To answer that, the pipeline analyzes:
1. Product characteristics (weight, visibility, price, fat content, category)
2. Outlet characteristics (size, type, location tier, establishment year)
3. Customer satisfaction (ratings)

---

## 2. Dataset Overview & Data Quality Report

The raw dataset (`data/BlinkIT Grocery Data.xlsx`) has **8,523 rows** and **13 columns**.

**Data quality issues found and fixed:**
* **Missing values:** `Item_Weight` had **1,463 missing values** (~17%). All other columns were fully populated.
* **Duplicates:** **0 duplicate rows.**
* **Inconsistent category labels:** `Item_Fat_Content` had `low fat` (112 rows), `Low Fat` (5,405 rows), and `Regular` (3,006 rows) — three labels for two categories.
* **Column naming:** the outlet identifier column was `'Outlet _Identifier'` (stray space).
* **Impossible values:** `Item_Visibility` had **526 rows** with `0.0`, which isn't physically possible for a product actively stocked on a shelf.
* **What "Sales" actually means:** `Sales` ranges from **$31.29 to $266.88** (mean **$140.99**) — this matches an individual product's **Maximum Retail Price (MRP)**, not a transaction total. There's no separate quantity-sold or store-revenue column. So throughout this project, **"Sales" = per-item price**, and "Total Sales" for a group = the summed price of every unit of that group in the dataset — not real revenue.

## 3. Data Cleaning & Preprocessing

Implemented in [`src/data_cleaning.py`](./src/data_cleaning.py):

1. **Column renaming:** `'Outlet _Identifier'` → `Outlet_Identifier`, `'Sr. no'` → `Sr_No`.
2. **Category standardization:** mapped `'low fat'` → `'Low Fat'` (and `'LF'`/`'reg'` variants, if present).
3. **Item Weight imputation (data-driven):**
   * First: mean weight of the *same* `Item_Identifier` at other outlets (a product's physical weight doesn't change by store).
   * Fallback: mean weight of the item's `Item_Type` category, for any products with no cross-outlet record.
4. **Item Visibility imputation:**
   * Converted impossible `0.0` values to `NaN`.
   * Same two-step imputation strategy as weight (same-product mean, then category mean).
5. **Data type correction:** `Sr_No` and `Outlet_Establishment_Year` cast to integers.
6. **Outlier scan (IQR method, informational only — not removed):**
   * Item Weight & Sales: 0 outliers.
   * Item Visibility: 144 outliers (1.69%) — likely high-prominence display placements, kept as valid.
   * Rating: 1,931 outliers (22.66%) — reflects genuine skew toward low customer scores.

Outputs: `data/cleaned_blinkit_data.csv`, `outputs/cleaned_data/cleaned_blinkit_data.csv`, `dashboard/data.json`, and `outputs/tables/outlier_summary.txt`.

## 4. Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

Generated by [`src/eda.py`](./src/eda.py), saved to [`outputs/figures/`](./outputs/figures):

* **Univariate:**
  * *Item Weight:* roughly uniform, 4.5–21.3 kg.
  * *Item Visibility:* right-skewed, most items sit at low visibility (~3–6%).
  * *Sales (price):* multi-modal — distinct pricing tiers roughly at $30–90, $90–135, $135–180, $180–266.
  * *Rating:* skewed toward high scores, mean ≈ 3.97.
* **Bivariate:**
  * *Sales vs. Weight / Visibility / Rating:* essentially no linear relationship — price is set independently of these.
  * *Sales by Outlet Type:* median prices are fairly consistent across Supermarket Type1/2/3 and Grocery Store formats.
* **Multivariate:**
  * The correlation heatmap shows near-zero linear correlation between all numeric features — product traits and store attributes are largely independent of each other.

## 5. Statistical Hypothesis Testing

From [`outputs/tables/statistical_tests.txt`](./outputs/tables/statistical_tests.txt):

| Test | Result | p-value | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| ANOVA: Sales vs. Item Type | F = 7.16 | 6.51 × 10⁻¹⁶ | **Significant.** Average price varies meaningfully by product category. |
| ANOVA: Sales vs. Outlet Type | F = 0.22 | 0.882 | Not significant. Store format doesn't affect average item price. |
| ANOVA: Sales vs. Outlet Size | F = 1.03 | 0.357 | Not significant. Store size doesn't affect average item price. |
| t-test: Sales vs. Fat Content | t = −0.56 | 0.576 | Not significant. Low Fat vs. Regular pricing is statistically indistinguishable. |

**Takeaway:** product category is the only tested grouping variable with a real effect on price; store-level attributes don't move it.

## 6. Business Questions & Answers

Full detail in [`outputs/tables/business_questions_answers.txt`](./outputs/tables/business_questions_answers.txt):

1. **Best-selling categories:** Fruits and Vegetables ($178,124) and Snack Foods ($175,434).
2. **Worst-selling categories:** Seafood ($9,078) and Breakfast ($15,597).
3. **Highest total-sales outlet type:** Supermarket Type1 ($787,550 — 65.5% of all sales).
4. **Highest average price per item by outlet type:** Supermarket Type2 ($141.68), just above Supermarket Type1 ($141.21).
5. **Best-performing location tier:** Tier 3 ($472,133 — 39.3% of total).
6. **Does outlet size matter?** Yes for total volume — Medium outlets carry the most items and highest total sales ($507,896) — but average price is flat across sizes.
7. **Does item visibility affect price?** No — correlation is essentially zero.
8. **Does MRP relate to Sales?** They're the same variable in this dataset (see §2), so trivially yes.
9. **Does fat content affect price?** Barely. Low Fat totals $776,320 (64.6%) vs. Regular $425,362 (35.4%), driven by stocking ratio, not price — average prices are nearly identical ($140.71 vs. $141.50).
10. **Highest-priced products:** FDR25, NCS29, FDK51 (avg ≈ $265).
11. **Lowest-priced products:** DRK12, FDG40, FDX59 (avg ≈ $33–34).
12. **Does rating relate to price?** No meaningful correlation.
13. **Does establishment year affect sales?** Outlets opened in **2018** have the highest total sales ($204,522), simply because that cohort stocks the largest catalog (1,463 items).
14. **Best-performing outlet combination:** Supermarket Type1 formats dominate the top combinations by total sales.

## 10. How to Run the Project

### Option A — Run the Python pipeline
```bash
cd BlinkIT-Grocery-Analytics
pip install -r requirements.txt

python src/data_cleaning.py   # 1. clean the raw data
python src/eda.py             # 2. generate all charts
python src/analysis.py        # 3. run stats, ML, and business-question answers
```
Each script depends on the previous one's output, so run them in order.

To explore interactively instead:
```bash
jupyter notebook notebooks/blinkit_eda.ipynb
```

### Option B — Run the interactive dashboard
The dashboard loads `data.json` via `fetch()`, which browsers block over `file://`. Serve it locally:
```bash
python3 -m http.server 8080
```
Then open **http://localhost:8080/dashboard/** in a browser and use the filters/search.

## 11. Business Recommendations

1. **Focus catalog investment on top categories.** Fruits & Vegetables, Snack Foods, Household, and Frozen Foods drive the majority of sales value — prioritize shelf space and promotion here over long-tail underperformers like Seafood.
2. **Replicate what's working at Supermarket Type1 outlets** — they lead on both total sales and per-item pricing consistency.
3. **Tier 3 locations show the strongest demand** — worth weighting toward Tier 3 in future outlet expansion.
4. **Don't rely on visibility to justify high prices.** Since visibility has no relationship with price or rating, use prominent shelf placement to promote high-margin items specifically, not just expensive ones.
5. **The Low Fat / Regular stocking split (65/35) looks intentional and reasonable** — pricing is nearly identical between the two, so the split is about demand mix, not pricing strategy.

---

## 12. Key Caveat to Remember

Because `Sales` = price-per-item rather than revenue, every "total sales" figure in this project is really a **sum of prices across however many units of that group appear in the dataset** — it reflects catalog composition as much as it reflects demand. Any future work with real transaction-level or quantity data would meaningfully change conclusions around "top-selling" categories or outlets.
